My manager once asked me, on the first day of Ramadan, completely seriously: "Will you still be able to write code? Or think? During the day?"

I laughed. It was a fair question. This is my attempt to answer it, and the others like it.

My own words, not a [fatwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatwa). If I get something wrong, that's on me.

---

## what is Ramadan? {#ramadan}

Ramadan is the ninth month of the Islamic lunar calendar. The dates shift every year because it follows the moon cycle. For the entire month, Muslims fast from the first light of dawn until sunset. No food, no water, no smoking. And yes, no sexual acts either. Before you ask, yes that's a real rule.

It's a direct order from God, one of the [five pillars of Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_pillars_of_Islam). An act of obedience, whether or not we fully understand why. But you feel it. By day twelve, 3pm hits differently — the coffee smell from someone else's mug, the slight fog behind your eyes, the way sunset starts to feel like a finish line. You pray more, give more to charity, spend more time with family. Most Muslims will tell you it's the most alive month of the year, even running on empty.

## why don't you drink? {#drinking}

It's forbidden in Islam. Alcohol is _haram_, prohibited. And beyond the religious ruling, the science isn't kind to it either.

I also try to avoid places where alcohol is the main point. Not always successfully. This is part of the rule too — not just "don't drink the thing." Islam understands that humans are weak and that [social pressure is real](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer_pressure). If you're in that environment often enough, you make it harder on yourself. The rule isn't just about the glass. It's about not putting yourself next to the glass in the first place.

### but I knew a Muslim who drinks?

Some drink knowing it's forbidden — that's between them and God, not my business. Some identify as Muslim culturally but don't practice strictly. Some are just figuring it out.

## why do you disappear for a few minutes? {#prayers}

Muslims pray five times a day: dawn, midday, afternoon, sunset, and night.

The midday and afternoon ones fall during the workday. Each takes about 5 to 10 minutes. I find a quiet spot, usually a corner of the stairwell or whatever meeting room nobody has booked, and pray.

## why don't you celebrate Christmas, Easter, [Midsommar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midsommar)...? {#holidays}

They're not my holidays.

I don't respond to "Merry Christmas" with "Merry Christmas" back. Muslims are accountable for their words, and saying it carries a theological claim I don't hold — that Jesus is the son of God, for example. So I keep it neutral. A smile, a nod.

Muslims have our own celebrations. [Eid al-Fitr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eid_al-Fitr) after Ramadan, and [Eid al-Adha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eid_al-Adha). Loud, warm, full of food, children get money. Arguably better.

As for the office Christmas party, I probably won't come. Unless not coming means getting fired, in which case I'll be there.
