AI models got better. I was getting worse.

I graduated June 2024 as a computer science engineer, right when GPT exploded onto the scene. My friends panicked that coding was dead. I was worried too. But I told myself I would use GPT as a teaching assistant, not a crutch. Smart, right?

Not really. Humans are lazy. Our brains optimize for energy savings, not growth. I started relying on AI for almost everything: my writing, my reading, my thinking. I stopped working through problems myself and just handed them off. The models kept improving. I kept drifting.

## The motivation

I started a new job where Go was the main stack. I tried learning it the usual way, official docs, random tutorials, but I always got bored and never finished a full course.

I had heard about Boot.dev from [ThePrimeagen](https://www.youtube.com/@ThePrimeTimeagen) and saw they had a Go course. I gave it a shot.

## Gamification of learning

I have no strong opinions on gamification in general, but for me it worked. The day streak and the dashboard made me not want to skip days. I finished 4 courses in about 40 days. What helped was not just the streak mechanic, the content itself was good. The two reinforced each other.

## Final thoughts

[Boot.dev](https://www.boot.dev/) convinced me to keep going. I am planning to do their DevOps track next, then backend. More importantly, it reminded me what it feels like to actually work through something, not skim a summary, not prompt an AI, but sit with it until it clicks. That is a habit worth rebuilding.
